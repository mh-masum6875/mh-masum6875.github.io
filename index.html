About

Research that has to work on real cameras

I like work where an idea has to survive contact with the real world. At Graymatics I build and ship video analytics: counting and classifying vehicles, checking safety and PPE compliance, spotting shoplifting, estimating the weight of sorted waste, and summarising long recordings so a reviewer can find the incident quickly. The pipelines run on more than 200 camera streams, so latency and reliability matter as much as accuracy.

My research asks the same question from the other side: how small can a model get before it stops being useful? My MSc looks at object detection on tiny wireless sensor devices, and I try to measure accuracy together with what it costs to run.

I also teach. I'm a part-time computing teacher at Abedeen International School in Putrajaya, and from 2023 to 2026 I tutored English at EF English Academy. Explaining an idea clearly is the fastest way I know to find out whether I really understand it.

Research

Efficient models for small devices

I try to treat cost as part of the design from the start, and to compare methods fairly: same data, same conditions, accuracy reported next to compute.

LightYolo-SSO

Also called WSN-YOLO

An object detector for wireless sensor nodes that have very little memory and power. Instead of shrinking an existing model, I searched for the architecture directly using Sperm Swarm Optimization, scoring each candidate on accuracy, FLOPs, size and speed.

Adaptive LightYolo-SSO

Early-exit inference

Most inputs are easy, so running the whole network on them wastes energy. I added two early exits to the LightYolo-SSO backbone. Easy samples leave early, and only the hard ones go all the way through.

98.6%of samples leave at the first exit
64.7%less compute on average
99.55%accuracy

The exit branches add 25,062 parameters, about 1.8% of the model. Savings are measured in operations, so real energy and latency still need testing on hardware.
